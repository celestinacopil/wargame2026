Darsenia / Cyber / Challenge 05

What the photo remembers

Available

Instructions

HQ has approved the technical analysis requested in report 7731-HUM-2026-114. Unit 7731 has copied the source's original photographs onto a forensic workstation.

A photo taken on a phone usually records when and where it was taken, in hidden data called EXIF metadata. Use the workstation's exiftool command to read it.

Find the GPS position where the photograph of the vessel was taken. According to the report, the vessel was about 300 m from the photographer.

Flag format: latitude and longitude in decimal degrees, rounded to 2 decimals: FLAG{latitude,longitude}, for example FLAG{12.34,56.78}

Evidence

  • Forensic workstation (terminal with the source's files)
  • Military intelligence report 7731-HUM-2026-114 (SECRET II, PDF)

Investigation guidance

  • New to the terminal? Type help, then ls to see the files. Read the note left by Unit 7731 with cat.
  • Not every copy of a photo keeps its metadata. Messaging apps often strip it when they compress an image.
  • GPS coordinates are shown in degrees, minutes and seconds by default. Convert them to decimal degrees (degrees + minutes/60 + seconds/3600), or check man exiftool for an option that does it for you.
  • The report warns that metadata can be faked. Does the position and time agree with what you found in challenge 03? The VesselTrace distance calculator can help you check.
